# My 2.0 Version

By 2030, Samyukta had built a career as a software engineer and cloud engineer at a leading MNC. What stood out about her was not just her ability to write code, but her curiosity about understanding how things worked behind the screen.

Over the years, she became particularly strong in concepts that many people found difficult, especially computer networks, application communication, system architecture, and what happens internally when an application runs. Instead of learning concepts only because they were part of a syllabus, she spent time understanding why things worked the way they did and how different parts of a system connected with each other.

Her journey was also shaped by the projects she built. Projects such as Ecomealz and DriveMentor started as college projects but eventually developed into proper working applications. Her projects focused on solving real problems, especially in areas such as sustainability, accessibility, and other SDG related goals. One of the applications she built received recognition because it went beyond being just an idea or a prototype and became a complete working software application that could actually help people.

Her GitHub portfolio showed the progress she had made as an engineer. It included projects that demonstrated her experience with different technologies and her ability to build applications from an idea into something functional. She also strengthened her cloud skills and gained practical experience in building, deploying, and working with applications in cloud environments. Her certifications and technical work added further proof to the skills she had developed.

Her career was not the only thing that changed. Samyukta continued to stay connected with the student community and contributed by mentoring students and sharing what she had learned. Having once been confused about placements, internships, projects, resumes, and which technical skills to focus on, she understood how useful proper guidance could be. She used her own experiences to help students who were going through the same stage she had once gone through.

Financial independence also became an important part of her life. Her career gave her the freedom to live independently and make decisions without constantly worrying about money. Earning well allowed her to take good care of herself while also giving her the freedom to spend on the people she loved and spoil them with things they wanted. For her, financial success was not only about having a good salary. It was about having security, freedom, and the ability to live a comfortable life.

She eventually built her life in either Coimbatore or Hyderabad, choosing a place that gave her both career opportunities and a better quality of life. She had found a balance between financial security and work life balance instead of measuring success only through salary or the size of the company she worked for.

Perhaps the most interesting part of Samyukta's journey was that her younger self had no clear idea about who she would eventually become. The Samyukta of 2026 was still figuring things out and was often unsure about where her efforts would lead. But she continued learning, experimenting, making mistakes, and putting in effort anyway.

The person she became was not created overnight. Samyukta 2.0 was built from all the experiences of Samyukta 1.0. The older version of her did not know exactly where she was going, but she kept moving forward, and that was what eventually brought her here.

# Reflection – Week 1

### Biggest insight I got about myself this week

I realized that I don't necessarily have a problem with knowing what I want to do. I actually have many goals and ideas. My bigger problem is trying to think about too many things at the same time. When I have college, placements, projects, DMI, career plans and personal things in my head together, I can feel overwhelmed and end up doing less than I expected. I realized that having a simple system is more useful for me than constantly making bigger plans.

### My biggest weakness/loop I noticed

My biggest weakness is inconsistency. Sometimes I get very motivated and want to improve everything at once. I make a big plan, but when I cannot follow it perfectly, I feel like I have fallen behind. Then I spend more time thinking about what I should have done instead of simply starting again. I noticed that I need to stop expecting myself to be perfect and focus more on showing up consistently.

### One system I will implement from this week (exact habit + time)

Every Sunday at 7:30 PM, I will spend 20 minutes planning the coming week.

I will write down my three most important tasks for the week and decide when I will work on them. I will also review the previous week and carry forward only the tasks that are actually important instead of creating an unnecessarily long list.
